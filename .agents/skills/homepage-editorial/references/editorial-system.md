# Editorial system

## Shared language

- Tone: quiet, precise, personal where appropriate, and free of marketing filler.
- Structure: title, one factual lede, then only the headings needed to expose the argument, workflow, or record.
- Claims: use measured verbs such as *records*, *describes*, *includes*, *is designed for*, or *is in development*. State conditions and limitations with the claim.
- Typography in Markdown: use ordinary headings, paragraphs, lists, emphasis, code, and links. Let the shared `.prose` component supply presentation; do not add presentational HTML or inline CSS.
- Metadata descriptions should be a one-sentence, concrete summary rather than a keyword list.

## Personal site

The portfolio speaks in first person about work, study, projects, and essays. Project pages make the relationship, role, and purpose clear in a few paragraphs. Essays may be more reflective, but their titles and descriptions remain specific. Work, personal projects, and research experiences use the established `extra.kind` model; do not turn a project page into a case-study template without a requested design change.

## Geometry of Meaning

Method pages say what changed, what was controlled, and what the measurement is intended to reveal. Observation pages name the immutable run, model, population, aggregation, and projection basis before drawing conclusions. Separate a result from its interpretation and its limits; never promote an embedding measurement to a claim about human language without support. Data pages document canonical membership and provenance, not corrections invented in the publishing repository.

## Bivouac

Write product documentation as practical technical orientation. Explain what the component does, where it sits in the Base → `.bivouac` → Field workflow, and its actual readiness. Use exact version names, supported capabilities, constraints, and source links. Avoid invented availability, compatibility, security, or offline claims.
